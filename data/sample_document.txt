# Introduction to Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

## 1. What is RAG?
Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a technique that combines information retrieval with large language models. It allows a system to retrieve relevant information from external documents before generating an answer. This helps provide more contextually accurate responses.

## 2. How RAG Works
A typical RAG system has two major phases: indexing and retrieval.

During indexing, documents are loaded, divided into smaller chunks, converted into numerical embeddings, and stored in a vector database.

During retrieval, a user's question is converted into an embedding. The system searches the vector database for similar chunks and retrieves the most relevant information.

## 3. Fixed-Size Chunking
Fixed-size chunking divides a document into pieces containing a specified number of characters or tokens. Each chunk has approximately the same size.

The main advantage is simplicity and predictable chunk sizes. However, a chunk may end in the middle of a sentence or paragraph, which can separate related information.

## 4. Overlapping Chunking
Overlapping chunking creates chunks that share some content with neighboring chunks. For example, a chunk may contain 500 characters while the next chunk begins 100 characters before the previous chunk ends.

This preserves context across chunk boundaries. However, it can create duplicate information and increase storage requirements.

## 5. Structure-Aware Chunking
Structure-aware chunking uses document organization, such as headings, paragraphs, and sections, to determine chunk boundaries.

It aims to keep related information together. This can improve contextual relevance, although document structures may vary and require additional processing.

## 6. Embeddings and Vector Databases
An embedding model converts text into a numerical vector. Similar meanings are represented by vectors that are close together in the embedding space.

FAISS is a library for efficient similarity search. It can store vectors and retrieve relevant entries based on similarity.

## 7. Evaluation of Chunking Strategies
Chunking strategies can be compared using retrieval relevance, answer relevance, context preservation, processing time, and the number of generated chunks.

A good strategy should retrieve useful information without creating unnecessarily large or fragmented chunks.

## 8. Applications of RAG
RAG can be used in document question-answering systems, educational assistants, customer support, enterprise knowledge search, and research tools.

## 9. Challenges and Future Scope
Important challenges include selecting suitable chunk sizes, preserving context, reducing retrieval noise, and evaluating answer quality.

Future improvements may include semantic chunking, hybrid search, reranking, and adaptive chunk sizes.

## 10. Conclusion
Document chunking is an important part of RAG systems. Fixed-size, overlapping, and structure-aware chunking each have advantages and limitations. Comparing them experimentally can help identify a suitable strategy for a particular application.
